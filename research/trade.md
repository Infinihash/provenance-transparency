# Crypto as trade settlement: references for Part 1 (2026-10-23)

Method note: compiled 2026-10-23 for the "crypto has become trade" section of the proposal. [V] = the page was fetched and the figure seen; [S] = seen only in a search extract or secondary write-up; [M] = from memory (none here). Several items are reporting from anonymous sources or economists' estimates and are marked as such. Numbers are cited in the page and README as [n].

1. Stablecoins moved about $46T in the 12 months to Oct 2025, or about $9T after removing bots and other non-organic activity (up 87%); the adjusted figure is more than half of Visa's volume and more than 5x PayPal's; supply above $300B. Source: a16z crypto, State of Crypto 2025, Oct 22 2025, https://a16zfrontier.com/posts/article/state-of-crypto-report-2025/ [V]
2. Adjusted stablecoin transaction value nearly $12.7T in Jan–Jun 2025, up more than 164% year on year; monthly transfers mostly above $2T. Source: Chainalysis, 2025 Geography of Crypto Report, Oct 2025, https://www.chainalysis.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/the-2025-geography-of-crypto-report-release.pdf [V]
3. Data from 33 payment firms: stablecoin payments at a $122B annual run rate in Aug 2025; B2B the largest category at $76B (up from $36B in Feb 2025); P2P $19B; average B2B transfer on Tron and Ethereum above $219,000. Source: Artemis, "Stablecoin Payments from the Ground Up", Oct 2025, https://reports.artemisanalytics.com/stablecoins/artemis-stablecoin-payments-from-the-ground-up-2025.pdf [V]
4. Counterpoint, same report: about $26T a year of adjusted on-chain stablecoin settlement, a large share of it trading, DeFi or MEV; the payments it could identify are about 1% of the total. [V]
5. Survey of 2,541 crypto users in Brazil, India, Indonesia, Nigeria and Turkey: among non-trading uses, 69% use stablecoins for currency substitution, 39% to pay for goods and services, 39% for cross-border payments; 47% save in dollars with them; Nigeria highest. Source: Castle Island / Brevan Howard Digital / Visa, "Stablecoins: The Emerging Market Story", Sep 12 2024, https://castleisland.vc/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/stablecoins_the_emerging_market_story_091224.pdf [V]
6. Non-crypto ("other") use about 50% of stablecoin transactions, roughly $2.5T a year; stablecoins still under 1% of US M2 and of spot FX. Source: Standard Chartered, "Stablecoins: The first killer app", late 2024, https://www.sc.com/en/uploads/sites/66/content/docs/sc-stablecoins-the-first-killer-app.pdf [V]
7. Chainalysis observes regular multi-million-dollar stablecoin transfers tied to Africa–Middle East–Asia trade in energy and merchant payments; Nigeria received $92.1B over 12 months; Chainalysis notes its exchange data misses B2B and OTC flows. Same source as [2]. [V]
8. Share of crypto activity on centralized exchanges: MENA 66%, Latin America 64%, Europe 53%, North America 49%; a third to a half happens elsewhere. Same source as [2]. [V]
9. Reporting, not confirmed fact: four anonymous sources say Russian oil firms use BTC, ETH and USDT to convert yuan and rupees for oil sold to China and India; "small but growing" against $192B of oil trade; one trader moving tens of millions of dollars a month. Source: Reuters, Mar 14 2025, https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/russia-leans-cryptocurrencies-oil-trade-sources-say-2025-03-14/ [V via search extract]
10. Reporting from unnamed sources: PDVSA, Venezuela's state oil company, moved crude and fuel sales to USDT under sanctions. Estimate, not confirmed: economist Asdrúbal Oliveros put about 80% of oil revenue in USDT (as reported by the WSJ; seen only in secondary write-ups). The US broadly eased PDVSA sanctions in Mar 2026. Sources: Reuters, Apr 22 2024, https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/venezuela-accelerate-cryptocurrency-shift-oil-sanctions-return-2024-04-22/ [S]; https://www.cointribune.com/en/venezuela-channels-80-of-oil-revenue-through-usdt-amid-sanctions/ (Dec 23 2025) [S, secondary]
11. Stablecoins are close to half of cross-border crypto flows across 184 countries; more closely linked to remittance costs than BTC or ETH; capital controls appear largely ineffective at curbing them (about $400B a year of cross-border USDT/USDC as cited by Artemis). Source: BIS Working Paper 1265, Auer/Lewrick/Paulick, May 8 2025, https://www.bis.org/publ/work1265.htm [V]

## What the evidence does not show

- Most on-chain stablecoin volume is still trading, DeFi and bot activity; identifiable payments are about 1% of settlement [4], and "adjusted" figures depend on each vendor's method.
- On-chain data cannot reliably tell whether a transfer is business-to-business; a wallet-to-wallet transfer may be an exchange, an OTC desk or one company's internal treasury. The regional exchange shares in [8] measure exchange activity only.
- Crypto oil settlement rests on anonymous sources [9] or economists' estimates [10], and is concentrated in sanctioned states, so it shows sanctions evasion more than mainstream trade.
- Survey samples are existing crypto users, not the general public or businesses [5]; the Artemis firm sample may double-count and is not complete [3].
- Stablecoins remain under 1% of global FX and money supply [6]. "Tipping point" describes growth and new rules, not overall market share.
